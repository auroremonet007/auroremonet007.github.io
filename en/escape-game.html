Case study · Personal project In progress

A city trail app, designed for my mother, a teacher

An escape game in the streets of Ollioules to learn by playing, which I design as a Product Owner and have built by an AI.

Setting
Personal project
Status
In design
My role
Product Owner: needs, design, specifications, prioritization
Development
Built by AI (Claude), driven by my specifications

Context

My mother is a teacher. She wanted her students to discover the village of Ollioules in a fun way, and to ground her lessons in the field. She had seen an app of this kind, but for another town.

The need

Explore a place by playing: a trail through the streets, with a puzzle to solve at each stop, to learn about the village's history and heritage. Two audiences stand out: a class with its teacher, and families visiting the village.

My approach

  1. 1

    Gather the need

    I talked with my mother about her teaching goals, observed how she prepares her school trips and studied the app that had inspired her.

  2. 2

    Scope a first version

    First goal: a complete escape game in Ollioules, with a trail through the village and questions at each stop.

  3. 3

    Work as a PO with an AI

    I design the features, break them down and specify them as I would for a development team. The AI (Claude) builds them, and I validate the result. It's a hands-on way to explore how AI changes the Product Owner role.

Planned scope for the first version

  1. 1

    A trail of stops through the village, with a map

  2. 2

    A puzzle at each stop to unlock the next one

  3. 3

    Hints when players get stuck

  4. 4

    A historical fact revealed after each correct answer

  5. 5

    A final screen summing up what players learned

Where the project stands

A first prototype exists. Next steps: finalize the trail and puzzles with my mother, then test the app in the field with a small group.

What I've learned so far

AI speeds up development a lot, but it doesn't replace the PO's work. The clearer and more granular my specifications, the better the result.
